## How to Use Cases

When facing a new user problem:

1. Find the closest case pattern below.
2. Transfer the method, not the historical surface.
3. Replace the original actors and variables with the user’s system.
4. Preserve the discipline: classify the system, integrate qualitative and quantitative evidence, design an execution apparatus, and define feedback.

## Case 1: Aerospace Overall Design Department

### Situation

Large aerospace and defense projects cannot be solved by one genius, one lab, or one discipline. A missile, satellite, or nuclear project involves propulsion, materials, control, computation, testing, manufacturing, logistics, safety, leadership, and long chains of interdependence. A local optimization in one subsystem can damage the whole project.

### System Judgment

This is a large engineered system with high coupling. It may not be as socially fluid as a society, but it is too complex for isolated departments.

### Method Used

Create an overall design department:

- chief designer or lead integrator
- subsystem experts
- shared technical language
- test and data system
- decision review mechanism
- final accountability by leadership

The overall design group does not replace leadership. It turns fragmented technical work into a coherent whole and gives leadership scientifically argued options.

### Transfer Rule

For any complex project, ask:

- Who owns the whole?
- Which subsystems must be coordinated?
- Where do local departments have incentives to optimize locally?
- What shared model or dashboard shows the whole state?
- What review mechanism catches cross-system failure?

Use this case for companies, product platforms, AI agent systems, infrastructure programs, research teams, and personal operating systems.

## Case 2: Price, Wage, and Fiscal Subsidy Policy Simulation

### Situation

In an economic reform problem, raising agricultural procurement prices improves farmer incentives, but retail prices, state subsidies, wages, market balance, fiscal deficits, money supply, savings, and investment all interact. Experts can propose reasonable opinions, but no single expert can fully calculate system consequences.

### System Judgment

This is an open complex giant system: many actors, many variables, changing behavior, policy feedback, and external constraints.

### Method Used

Use qualitative-to-quantitative metasynthetic work:

1. Gather economists, department managers, statisticians, and system engineers.
2. Let experts form qualitative judgments about the real problem.
3. Convert the problem into variables, constraints, policy levers, and outputs.
4. Build a model using real statistical data.
5. Simulate multiple policy schemes.
6. Return results to experts for criticism.
7. Revise assumptions and model structure.
8. Produce several policy options with consequences and tradeoffs.

### Transfer Rule

When a decision has many stakeholders and delayed consequences, do not stop at “experts think X.” Turn expert judgment into scenarios and variables.

Use this case for pricing, compensation, growth strategy, public policy, product monetization, organizational restructuring, AI deployment, and investment decisions.

## Case 3: Systems Seminar as Knowledge Production

### Situation

Creating a new discipline or solving a frontier problem requires input from many fields. No single researcher can hold all relevant knowledge. If discussions are informal and unstructured, insights dissipate.

### System Judgment

This is a knowledge-production system. The core resource is not only information but organized intellectual interaction.

### Method Used

Create a recurring seminar mechanism:

- invite experts from different domains
- let one person present deeply
- let participants question assumptions
- have a lead integrator summarize and connect the discussion
- record unresolved problems
- continue through letters, small groups, and follow-up work

The seminar is not a meeting for status reporting. It is a cognitive engine.

### Transfer Rule

For any hard intellectual problem, design the discussion system:

- What knowledge must enter the room?
- Who can challenge the current frame?
- Who synthesizes?
- How are unresolved questions tracked?
- How does the discussion become models, experiments, decisions, or writing?

Use this case for research programs, strategy rooms, product discovery, executive decision forums, and learning communities.

## Case 4: Artificial Intelligence and Foreign Method Adoption

### Situation

A technical field becomes globally fashionable. Many researchers chase a method because foreign labs are investing heavily in it. The method may be promising, but its theoretical foundation and applicability are unclear.

### System Judgment

This is a science-and-technology strategy system. It includes knowledge uncertainty, national or company resource allocation, hype cycles, and imitation risk.

### Method Used

Adopt a boundary-checking stance:

- Study foreign work seriously.
- Ask what problem it actually solves.
- Ask what assumptions it depends on.
- Ask whether the target system is simple, large, simple giant, or open complex.
- Keep useful methods as partial inputs.
- Do not let a foreign method become the whole strategy.

### Transfer Rule

When a user asks about a trend, do not answer with adoption or rejection only. Produce a fit analysis:

- what the method is good for
- what it cannot handle
- what evidence is missing
- how to test it locally
- how to combine it with other methods

Use this case for AI tools, management frameworks, foreign policy models, growth tactics, investment theses, and new research paradigms.

## Case 5: Traditional Chinese Medicine as Qualitative System Experience

### Situation

Traditional medical practice accumulates long-term clinical experience. It observes the body as a whole, but its language may not map directly to modern scientific variables. Rejecting it loses experience; accepting it uncritically blocks scientific transformation.

### System Judgment

The human body is an open complex giant system. Some knowledge about it exists as tacit, qualitative, pattern-based experience.

### Method Used

Treat traditional experience as qualitative input:

- preserve clinical observations and pattern recognition
- separate useful experience from untested explanation
- translate patterns into testable variables where possible
- compare with modern biological, physiological, psychological, and clinical data
- iteratively build a better system model

### Transfer Rule

When a system contains tacit knowledge, do not discard it because it is not yet formal. Also do not make it sacred. Use it as a source of hypotheses and pattern recognition.

Use this case for expert craft, sales intuition, founder judgment, clinical practice, teaching experience, investment pattern recognition, and operations know-how.

## Case 6: Geography and Environmental Construction

### Situation

A regional environmental project, such as river protection, urban planning, desert treatment, or infrastructure development, touches forestry, water, agriculture, transportation, finance, energy, ecology, local livelihoods, and government departments. If each department handles its slice separately, the whole system can fail.

### System Judgment

This is an open complex giant system with natural, social, economic, and institutional layers.

### Method Used

Use system-level geographic construction:

- define the region as one interacting system
- map natural flows: water, land, climate, ecology, resources
- map social flows: people, industry, transport, capital, governance
- identify cross-department coupling
- build phased plans with feedback indicators
- prevent department-by-department fragmentation

### Transfer Rule

For any multi-domain transformation, avoid cutting by administrative convenience if the real system crosses boundaries.

Use this case for city strategy, company transformation, platform ecosystems, supply chains, climate adaptation, education reform, and health systems.

## Case 7: High-Level Consultation Is Not an Opinion Collection

### Situation

A consulting group collects many expert essays or recommendations about transportation, industry, education, or company strategy. Each opinion is valuable but partial. A binder of opinions does not equal a decision.

### System Judgment

This is a decision-support system with fragmented expert knowledge.

### Method Used

Upgrade consultation into metasynthetic work:

1. Gather expert opinions.
2. Extract assumptions, variables, and proposed levers.
3. Build a shared model or comparison matrix.
4. Insert actual data.
5. Run scenarios or structured evaluations.
6. Return results to experts.
7. Revise until the group reaches a more comprehensive judgment.

### Transfer Rule

Whenever the output is only “many people said many things,” synthesize:

- where experts agree
- where they conflict
- what each expert sees that others miss
- what variables decide the dispute
- what experiment or model can move the issue forward

Use this case for board strategy, investment committees, product research synthesis, technical architecture debates, and policy consultation.

## Case 8: Workshop Hall System

### Situation

Open complex systems require more than a meeting. They require experts, knowledge bases, data systems, simulation tools, AI assistance, visualization, and live interaction. The problem statement itself may change during inquiry.

### System Judgment

This is a human-machine cognitive system. The design object is the thinking environment.

### Method Used

Build a workshop hall:

- experts bring domain judgment
- databases and documents provide memory
- models and simulations provide consequence testing
- AI and software activate information and generate alternatives
- visualization makes system state inspectable
- human debate resolves meaning and value
- feedback changes the question, model, and plan

### Transfer Rule

For any complex recurring decision, design the room, tools, cadence, and roles. Do not rely on ad hoc chat.

Use this case for executive war rooms, AI-assisted research, crisis response, product strategy, national policy, and personal knowledge systems.

## Case 9: Internet as an Open Complex Giant System

### Situation

The internet is not just cables and computers. It includes users, platforms, protocols, software, content, economic incentives, governance, security, culture, and feedback loops. Adding users changes the system. New behavior emerges.

### System Judgment

This is an open complex giant system with technical, social, economic, and cognitive layers.

### Method Used

Analyze internet-like systems by layers:

- infrastructure
- protocol and platform
- user behavior
- information flow
- trust and security
- business incentives
- governance
- emergent risks

Human-machine collaboration matters because machines process information but people define purpose, meaning, trust, and judgment.

### Transfer Rule

For any digital ecosystem, avoid treating it as only a technology stack. Include behavior, incentives, information quality, governance, and human cognition.

Use this case for AI platforms, social networks, marketplaces, developer ecosystems, data platforms, and knowledge-management systems.

## Case 10: Validity Period of Complex-System Decisions

### Situation

A model or plan for an open complex system works under current conditions. Over time, actors adapt, variables change, external shocks appear, and the model loses validity.

### System Judgment

Open complex systems are time-sensitive. Their structure and parameters evolve.

### Method Used

Treat every solution as valid within a decision window:

- state assumptions
- define observation metrics
- specify early-warning signals
- set review cadence
- revise the model when conditions change
- avoid once-for-all theory

### Transfer Rule

For any strategy under uncertainty, include an expiration date or review trigger.

Use this case for market strategy, policy design, AI governance, health plans, learning plans, business operations, and long-term research agendas.

## Case Selection Map

- If the user needs **coordination across many parts**, use Case 1.
- If the user needs **decision under many variables**, use Case 2.
- If the user needs **knowledge creation**, use Case 3.
- If the user asks about **a trend or imported method**, use Case 4.
- If the user has **tacit expert knowledge**, use Case 5.
- If the problem crosses **natural/social/technical domains**, use Case 6.
- If the input is **many expert opinions**, use Case 7.
- If the task recurs and needs **tools plus experts**, use Case 8.
- If the object is **digital ecosystem/AI/platform/network**, use Case 9.
- If the strategy will face **changing conditions**, use Case 10.
