## 1. Use Method, Not Persona

This skill is not a roleplay style. It should not imitate Qian Xuesen’s identity or claim certainty about what he would say. It should operationalize patterns from his systems thinking for any system object:

- system classification before solution design
- open complex giant systems as a distinct object
- qualitative-to-quantitative metasynthetic method
- overall design department as decision-support organization
- human-machine collaboration with humans as the lead
- dynamic feedback instead of once-for-all conclusions

## 2. Generalization Rule

Apply the method to any system by translating the object into five universal dimensions:

1. **Boundary**: what is inside, what is outside, and what crosses the boundary.
2. **Subsystems**: actors, modules, functions, resources, institutions, tools, or organs.
3. **Relations**: flows of information, energy, money, material, attention, trust, incentives, or control.
4. **State variables**: what describes system health or performance at a given moment.
5. **Feedback loops**: how actions change the environment, which then changes the system.

Examples of translation:

- A company is a system of strategy, product, customers, people, capital, operations, culture, and external markets.
- A product is a system of users, jobs-to-be-done, workflows, interface, data, infrastructure, distribution, and feedback.
- A personal growth plan is a system of goals, energy, time, habits, learning loops, environment, incentives, and identity.
- An AI agent workflow is a system of intent, context, tools, models, memory, verification, cost, and human oversight.
- A city is a system of population, land, transport, housing, economy, ecology, governance, and public services.
- A research program is a system of questions, evidence, methods, instruments, peers, funding, and theory-building.

Do not force every object to become a national-policy analogy. Preserve the scale and nature of the user’s system.

## 3. System Classification

Classify the object before selecting tools:

- **Simple system**: few variables, clear causal relations. Direct analysis is usually enough.
- **Large system**: many variables but reasonably clear structure. Use modeling, decomposition, and coordination.
- **Simple giant system**: huge number of subsystems but limited subsystem types. Statistical or synergetic methods may work.
- **Open complex giant system**: many subsystems, many subsystem types, multiple layers, changing structure, nonlinear relationships, and exchange with the external environment. Examples: society, organizations, cities, markets, education systems, human health, AI ecosystems, internet platforms, large strategic transformations.

If the system is open and complex, avoid pretending that a single formula, benchmark, model, or expert can solve it.

## 4. From Qualitative to Quantitative

The core method is not “qualitative plus quantitative” but “from qualitative to quantitative”:

1. Gather qualitative judgments from experts, field workers, users, history, and practical cases.
2. Identify the implicit variables and causal assumptions behind those judgments.
3. Convert what can be converted into indicators, constraints, scenarios, models, or experiments.
4. Run calculations, simulations, comparisons, or structured evaluations where possible.
5. Return results to experts and stakeholders for critique.
6. Iterate until the recommendation is robust enough for the current decision window.

Do not force fake precision. Mark unquantified assumptions clearly.

## 5. Overall Design Department Thinking

For large tasks, design an “overall design department.” For non-organizational systems, translate this into the minimum apparatus needed to observe, model, decide, execute, and correct the system:

- **Decision owner**: who ultimately decides.
- **Chief designer / lead integrator**: who owns synthesis.
- **Domain experts**: people with deep, partial knowledge.
- **Data and evidence system**: statistics, documents, logs, research, user evidence, market evidence.
- **Model/tool system**: spreadsheets, simulations, AI tools, databases, prototypes, experiments.
- **Review mechanism**: structured debate and revision.
- **Feedback mechanism**: metrics and cadence after execution begins.

This is a consulting and design apparatus. It supports judgment but does not replace accountable leadership.

## 6. Human-Machine Collaboration

Use AI and software as part of the system:

- machines are good at retrieval, calculation, simulation, pattern discovery, drafting, and consistency checks
- humans are needed for framing, values, experience, tacit knowledge, contradiction resolution, and final judgment
- the goal is human-machine intelligence, not machine-only intelligence

In outputs, specify which parts should be done by people, which by tools, and where human review is mandatory.

## 7. Dynamic Feedback

Open complex systems change. Any recommendation should include:

- what to observe
- what metrics to track
- what early warnings matter
- when to revisit assumptions
- what condition triggers a pivot

Avoid presenting long-term predictions as permanent truth.

## 8. Anti-Patterns

Avoid these failure modes:

- using trendy foreign methods without checking fit
- treating expert opinion as complete truth
- treating quantitative models as objective truth
- reducing social/organizational problems to one or two variables
- making a grand philosophical statement without an execution mechanism
- confusing “holistic” with vague
- confusing “complex” with unsolvable
- creating a plan without feedback loops
- creating an AI-heavy plan without human accountability
